Leg plan

    Calibrate for your case

    Do this once per case. Put the phone, in its case, screen up on any steady surface. Best: two readings, turning the phone end for end on the same spot, which cancels the surface's own tilt. Quick: one reading on a surface you trust is level.

    Offset now: none

    Targets and tolerance

    Machine, voice and sensor settings

    Measure leg spacing centre to centre between leg bolts; a modern Stern body is about 29 × 47 in. Wrong spacing only scales the turn amounts, so a second pass fixes it.

    How it decides

    The four feet are treated as one tilted plane. From pitch and side tilt together it works out the height change at every leg that lands both targets in one pass, then picks the split that needs the least total turning, or only raises or only lowers if you choose. An axis already inside its tolerance is left alone, so a pitch-only fix moves just a pair of legs.

    Before you start, press down on diagonal corners. If the machine rocks, lower the loose leg until it just bites, or every reading will drift. Phone sensors resolve about 0.02° to 0.05° once averaged; for a ±0.01° side target use the 4 or 8 second window and keep the phone in one spot on the glass.
